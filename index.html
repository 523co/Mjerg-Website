Getting it

Added from inside Osmium

There's no invite link to click. Osmium handles it in the app, which is three steps and takes about ten seconds:

  1. Open Osmium

    Desktop, web or mobile, all the same.

  2. Click on Mjerg

    Search for it, or click the name anywhere you see the bot posting.

  3. Hit "Add to Chat"

    Pick the community, confirm the permissions, done. You'll need Manage community there to be allowed to add it.

What's in it

Moderation that keeps a record

Warnings, bans and purges all leave a trail you can look up later. Automod handles the boring half, spam and invites and mass mentions, before anyone has to type a command. And !snipe exists for when someone deletes something a half-second too late.

  • 19 commands
  • !warn, !warnings, !modlog
  • Automod rules you toggle individually
  • Snipe and editsnipe

The one people stay for

Auto-translation, running in the background

Point Mjerg at a channel and it translates messages in and out of any language as people post. Someone writes in Portuguese, everyone else reads it in their own language, and the replies go back the other way. No copying text into a translator, no command to remember, nobody switching to English out of politeness.

!autotranslate #channel es turns it on for a channel and the same command turns it off. !translate covers the one-off case.

  • Any language, both directions
  • Set per channel, not per community
  • Works on ordinary messages, no tagging
  • Off again with one command

Levelling and an economy people bother with

XP from talking, roles that unlock at a level you pick, a leaderboard. Coins from !work, spent in a shop you stock yourself, lost at !gamble shortly after. Both work out of the box and both can be turned off.

  • 5 levelling commands, 24 fun ones
  • Level roles via !levelrole
  • Shop, balances, transfers
  • Wordle, hangman, trivia, counting

Utilities you'd otherwise need four bots for

Reminders, weather, a calculator, unit conversion, dictionary and Wikipedia lookups, tags, AFK, encoders and hashes. None of them are impressive on their own. Together they are the reason nobody has to leave the app to get something done.

  • 20 utility commands
  • !remind, !translate, !weather
  • Per-community tags
  • Per-member AFK notes

Setup that stays in chat

Nine setup commands cover the whole thing: log and welcome channels, auto-roles, reaction roles, sticky messages, custom triggers. !config shows you every setting at once, which beats hunting through a web panel for the one you changed.

  • 9 setup commands
  • Reaction roles and auto-roles
  • Sticky messages and triggers
  • Everything visible in !config
